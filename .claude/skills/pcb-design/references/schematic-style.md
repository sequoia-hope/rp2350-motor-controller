# Phase 2: schematic style

A schematic is a document for humans. Someone opening it should be able to follow power and signals from the connectors to the loads without searching. If it looks like it was generated by a script, it fails, no matter how correct the netlist is.

## Sheet organization

- **Small boards (< ~40 parts)**: one sheet, divided into labeled regions (graphic boxes with a title like "3.3 V BUCK").
- **Larger boards**: hierarchical sheets, one per functional block from the block diagram. Top sheet shows the blocks and how they connect, roughly matching the block diagram.
- Order regions/sheets in signal-flow order: inputs and power entry on the left/first, processing in the middle, outputs on the right/last.
- Title block filled in: board name, revision, date, author, sheet title.

## Within a block

- **Flow left to right.** Inputs enter from the left, outputs leave to the right.
- **Voltage top to bottom.** Higher voltages toward the top, ground at the bottom. Power symbols point up, ground symbols point down. Never a ground symbol pointing up.
- **Wires, not labels, inside a block.** If two pins are on the same sheet and near each other, connect them with a wire. Labels are for connections between blocks or sheets, or to avoid a wire crossing a whole page.
- **Local/net labels for cross-block nets on one sheet; hierarchical or global labels only between sheets.** A global label used where a wire would do is a style failure.
- **Supporting parts sit next to the pin they serve.** Decoupling caps drawn at the IC's power pin. Pull-ups at the pin they pull. Feedback divider next to the FB pin. Crystal and load caps at the oscillator pins.
- **Decoupling caps**: draw them next to the power pin, vertical, between the rail and ground. A short column of caps beside the IC is fine; a separate "decoupling" section across the page is not.
- **Minimize crossings and bends.** No wires running through symbols or text. No four-way junctions (use two T's offset).
- **Spacing**: give symbols room to breathe. Values and references must not overlap anything. Every part's value and reference readable without zoom.

## Symbols

- Pins arranged by function, not by package pin number: inputs and control left, outputs right, power top, ground bottom. If the library symbol is pin-number ordered (common for MCUs and connectors from generators), make a project-local symbol that's arranged properly. For big MCUs, split into units (power, GPIO bank A, peripherals).
- Connectors: pin order matches the physical connector so the schematic can be checked against a wiring diagram. Label each pin with its function next to it.
- Every part has a meaningful value: "10k", "100n 50V X7R", "DRV8353RS". Not "R" or "C".

## Annotations

Humans annotate schematics. Add short text notes for:
- non-obvious choices: "Rsense 2 mΩ: 30 A peak, 1.8 W, see parts/rsense.md"
- layout-critical things: "Place C12 within 2 mm of U3 pin 7"
- DNP options and jumpers: what they're for
- test points
- rail voltages and current at the source

## Net naming

Name every net that leaves a block or matters for routing: `+12V`, `+3V3`, `VBUS`, `MOTOR_A`, `I2C_SDA`, `SW_BUCK`. Unnamed auto-nets are fine only for local connections inside a block. These names feed `nets.csv` in phase 3.

## Automated style check (`scripts/schematic_style_check.py`)

Fails on:
- more than N global labels relative to the number of inter-sheet connections
- labels connecting two pins that are within X mm of each other on the same sheet (should be a wire)
- symbols arranged on a regular grid (detect: x and y coordinates of symbol origins clustering on a small set of evenly spaced values)
- decoupling caps whose nearest IC power pin (same net) is more than X mm away
- ground symbols rotated non-down, power symbols rotated non-up
- overlapping text, text over wires
- missing values, missing footprints, missing MPN/LCSC fields

The script catches the mechanical failures. The visual review catches the rest.

## Visual review

Export the schematic to PDF/SVG and render to PNG. The reviewer (stronger model, fresh context) answers:
1. Can I follow the main power path from input connector to every load?
2. Can I follow each signal from source to destination?
3. Does any region look like a grid or a list rather than a circuit?
4. Is every passive next to what it serves?
5. Is anything cramped, overlapping or hard to read?
6. Would a senior engineer be comfortable presenting this in a design review?
