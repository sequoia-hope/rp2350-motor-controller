Space Budget — Smaller Parts & JLCPCB Process Options

Every footprint on the rev-B board asked whether a smaller, buyable part does the same job · what JLCPCB will build for free that the design rules don't use yet (capped via-in-pad, 4-mil traces, 0.2 mm vias) · branch rev-b-fixes @ ed7808b · 2026-09-01

In one paragraph. The board is 77.05 × 46.45 mm (3,579 mm²), 323 footprints, 617 vias. Connectors own 43 % of the top side outright and are an interface promise, so the recoverable space is elsewhere: on the bottom side, where the four half-bridge driver blocks, three current-sense amplifiers, the buck and the isolated CAN transceiver are all in packages two to four times larger than in-stock alternatives, and on the top side in 16 DNP snubber footprints that have been empty since rev A, six 2512 shunts and four SMA phase clamps. Adding up only the changes with no electrical downside (Tier A, Tier B) and the three architectural swaps worth doing (C1–C3) frees about 230 mm² of top-side and 600 mm² of bottom-side courtyard — the bottom side drops from 1,523 to ~920 mm² of parts, −40 % — plus ~120 mm² of pure placement freedom from courtyard margins that are twice JLCPCB's own spacing minimum. On the process side the single most valuable fact is that JLCPCB caps and fills via-in-pad for free on 6-layer boards (this board is 6-layer) and the design rules sit at 0.16/0.18 mm on a fab that builds 0.09/0.09 — the "topological wall" that F-44 measured is a rule-pitch wall, and the rule pitch can drop from 0.38 to 0.20 mm without paying anything. Blind/buried vias are not on offer; don't plan around them.

4 · Tier C — architectural swaps

C1 · Gate-driver block → LMG1205 (−306 mm², −24 parts, all bottom side)

Every one of the four half-bridge blocks today is 99.7 mm² of bottom-side courtyard around a SOIC-8 EG3113: the driver (39.8), a bootstrap diode + 4.7 Ω (14.3), two 0 Ω + two anti-parallel SOD-523 diodes for fast turn-off (15.5), three 10.2 Ω gate resistors (12.8), and a PNP + 100 Ω low-side turn-off booster (17.3). Most of that network exists because the EG3113's sink is weak. The TI LMG1205 (C2653814, 1,028 @ $2.55) is a 100 V half-bridge driver in a 2.0 × 2.0 mm DSBGA-12 that was designed for exactly the operating point this board is already at:

  • VDD 4.5–5.5 V — it runs from Vdrive (5.04 V) as-is; no UVLO gamble.
  • Integrated 100 V bootstrap diode with a 5 V clamp → D3/D10/D14/D18 and R9/R25/R41/R73 go.
  • Split outputs (LOH/LOL, HOH/HOL) → separate turn-on and turn-off resistors, so the 0 Ω + diode pairs and the PNP booster go, and F-41's gate-resistor sweep gets independent on/off knobs for free.
  • 1.2 A source / 5 A sink, 35 ns propagation, TTL inputs (3.3 V-compatible), independent HI/LI — same control model as today.
  • All 12 balls are on the perimeter of the 4 × 4 grid (A1–A4, B1, B4, C1, C4, D1–D4; A3/C4 and C1/D4 are internally paired), so every ball escapes outward — no between-ball routing at 0.4 mm pitch.

Per bridge the block becomes ~6 mm² (BGA) + four 0603 gate resistors (17.1) + the bootstrap cap (4.28, unchanged) ≈ 27 mm²: −73 mm² per bridge, −292 mm² for four, and 11 parts out / 5 in per bridge (−24 net). Cost +$8.4/board (4 × $2.55 − 4 × $0.45 − removed passives).

What it locks in. VDD max is 5.5 V. Choosing the LMG1205 makes the 5 V gate drive permanent and kills F-41's last-resort 10–12 V rail — the same ceiling the fourteen TS5A3159s (5.5 V max) already impose, so nothing new is lost, but it should be said out loud. Needs Standard PCBA (≥ 0.35 mm pitch; JLCPCB adds X-ray automatically) and 0.1 mm traces from the 0.2 mm ball pads, i.e. the design-rule move in §6. Bench-check the 5 V clamp behaviour with a silicon FET's larger Qg before committing four of them.

Alternatives ranked. LM5113 (WSON-10 4 × 4, C2677036, 821 @ $4.09) is the same feature set in a leaded package: −60 mm²/bridge, no BGA. UCC27282DRCR (VSON-10 3 × 3, 1,797 @ $3.29, 5.5–16 V, integrated bootstrap) is the right part only if the 10–12 V rail lands: its VDD UVLO rising threshold is 4.7–5.4 V, so at 5.04 V a fraction of units never start. F-40 note: the JLC in-stock mirror shows EG3113 C480666 at 965 units / $0.45 today against the 9 units recorded on 2026-08-11 — re-verify F-40 before treating the driver swap as forced; as a space decision it stands either way.

C2 · Buck → 80 V synchronous (−70 mm², bottom side)

U28 XL7005A (150 kHz, 0.4 A, async) + L3 47 µH 5 × 5 + D4 SS210 (SMA) + C11 100 µF electrolytic = 132 mm². A synchronous 80 V part at ≥ 400 kHz — LMR38010SDDAR (80 V in, 1 A, HSOIC-8, C5219310, 2,014 @ $0.92) — deletes the catch diode and the electrolytic and drops the inductor to 22–33 µH in a 4 × 4 case with 2 × 22 µF 0805 ceramics: ~60 mm², −2 parts. It also retires F-29 (0.45 A budget vs a 0.4 A part, inductor near saturation). Pick the FPWM/PFM variant deliberately and re-check the 5.04 V set point; the buck module is one of F-44's zero-slack blocks.

C3 · Encoder switch pairs → dual SPDT (−34 mm², encoder fabric)

Four pairs are already neighbours on B.Cu: U13+U14 (EXT_ANALOG_SW, 3 mm apart), U24+U25 (EXT_ANALOG_SW, 3 mm), U22+U26 (H1_SW / H2_SW, 2.8 mm), U23+U27 (H3_SW / I2C_SW, 2.8 mm). Each pair → one TS5A23159RSER (dual SPDT, independent controls, UQFN-10 1.5 × 2.0 mm, C138710, 1,112 @ $0.91): 53.6 → ~20 mm². U30–U33 (terminations) stay single — they sit at their own transceiver by design and a shared quad would put the 100 Ω termination at the end of a stub. The WCSP TS5A3159AYZPR (1.4 × 0.9 mm) exists but at 335 units it can't cover 14 per board.

C4 · Shunts 2512 → 2010 (−76 mm², top side)

The two-shunts-per-phase scheme is the 10 A precision mode, so six parts stay six. A 2010 2 W 8 mΩ dissipates 0.8 W at 20 A (each shunt carries 10 A): 40 % of rating, same margin class as today's 3 W 2512 at 27 %. 1206 1 W parts (−130 mm²) would run at rating — no. Keep the Kelvin taps.

C5 · Bulk caps 3 → 2 (−86 mm², top side) — a decision, not a recommendation

C39/C42/C80 are 100 µF/100 V polymer Ø10 × 12 (C2887236). Dropping one takes the regen headroom from 0.22 J to 0.15 J between 60 V and TVS breakdown; F-28's bus guard and chopper are what make that survivable. No smaller 100 V/100 µF case is stocked.

5 · Interfaces — listed for scale, not proposed

Connectors are 1,640 mm² (46 % of the board). None of these is a component choice in the sense above; each changes what the board mates with.

  • J7/J8 interboard serial (213 mm²) — the horizontal 2.54 mm header/socket pair is the largest non-power item on the board. A 1.27 mm horizontal pair is ≈ −140 mm²; giving up direct board-to-board mating for a cable pair (JST SH horizontal like J4/J6) ≈ −124 mm². Worth a decision if the side-by-side pitch is not yet promised.
  • J11 CAN (76 mm²) 2.54 mm vertical → JST GH/SH ≈ −50; J5 E-stop (58) → SH ≈ −14.
  • Mounting holes (167 mm²): #6 Plastite Ø3.9 → M2.5 ≈ −55; M3 barely moves it.
  • XT30 ×4 (892) and J12 (217): the adapter-PCB promise in the README depends on them — untouched.
  • J3 USB-C (95, bottom): 6-pin receptacles are 3 A parts; the 5 A PD contract (F-27) needs the 16-pin's paralleled VBUS/GND pins — keep.

7 · Suggested order

  1. Design rules first — 0.10/0.10 signal class, 0.2/0.35 vias, and the POFV decision. Every placement tool on this repo is keyed to rule pitch and courtyards; changing them after re-running morph/creep/field wastes the runs.
  2. Tier A (A1–A3, A5) with the existing scripts, then A4 as a library edit.
  3. B1, B4–B6 — value/footprint swaps, no symbol work.
  4. B2 + B3 — new symbols/footprints; both land in the zero-slack bottom-side blocks, and B3 also resolves a possibly-unbuyable U16.
  5. C1 bundled with the F-40/F-41 decision; C2 bundled with F-29; C3.
  6. C4, C5 and anything in §5 only as product decisions.
