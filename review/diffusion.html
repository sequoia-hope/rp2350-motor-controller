Diffusion Placement Counterpoint

Zero-shot fresh placement of the 49 morph parts by a pretrained chip-placement diffusion model (Lee et al., ICML 2025 / arXiv 2407.12282) · counterpoint to the jiggle2 creep morph · hub

The creep stepper morphs the shipped rev-A placement continuously toward the rev-B targets and stalls at mean u ≈ 0.435 against a topological wall. This experiment asks the opposite question: forget continuity — if a generative placer lays these parts out fresh in the same region, with the same fixed context, how does its answer compare with the hand-designed rev-B target placement?

Setup: the authors' released Large+v2 checkpoint (6.3M params, trained purely on synthetic netlists), run zero-shot with their universal-guidance sampling (legality + HPWL potentials, Lagrangian weighting). The morph region becomes a 20.7 × 29.3 mm canvas with 49 movable parts, 32 fixed blockers (back-side courtyards; THT connectors block only at their pins — the rev-B design itself tucks parts under the J2/J12 bodies), and 27 pinned net terminals. GND is exempt, exactly like the morph's connectivity rule.

Reading

The region has materially shorter-wire placements than rev-B, and the model finds them zero-shot. All 16 raw samples beat the rev-B target on wirelength (0.74–0.82×), and s09 does it courtyard-clean under the same rectangle model that scores the rev-B target exactly legal — an 18% HPWL reduction with zero overlap. Most other samples carry a handful of residual overlaps (worst ≈1 mm); that is expected for raw guided samples — the authors run a separate legalization pass for their benchmark numbers, which this experiment deliberately skips to see the model's own output.

These are different layouts, not perturbations. Mean part travel from rev-A shipped is 9–12 mm — the model rearranges the region wholesale. Note the anchors' own numbers: rev-B target HPWL (2301) is essentially rev-A's (2324) — the rev-B edit inserts the termination switches with minimal disruption; it never tried to shorten wire. The diffusion answer sits in a different basin entirely, which is precisely the kind of placement the creep morph cannot reach: creep is homotopy-constrained by construction, and its s = 0.435 wall is a property of the path to this target, not of the region's capacity.

Caveats before falling in love. HPWL over a star netlist (GND exempt) is a placement proxy, not routability: two copper layers, pours, the pin-protrusion realities under J2/J12, and the encoder differential pairs all live outside the model. No rotation either — every part keeps its shipped orientation. The honest use of s09 is as a counterpoint existence proof (the wall is about the target, not the territory) and possibly as a seed for a rev-C hand layout — not as a drop-in placement.

Method notes

  • Objects are courtyard bounding boxes; pins are pad centres, star topology per net from the driving terminal (external nets terminate in a virtual port clamped to the canvas edge, partitioning-style).
  • Fixed context is pinned via the model's port mask (inpainting-style: those coordinates never denoise), but a one-line patch passes a separate legality_mask to the guidance potential so fixed blockers still repel movers — upstream excludes all pinned objects from legality.
  • Pad blockers are grandfathered by shipping: where the rev-B design places a courtyard inside a THT pad's halo, the blocker shrinks to fit — the reference placement scores exactly legal by construction.
  • The footprint view is cosmetic: pads and fab/silk body outlines are read from the board (footgeom.py) and drawn at each placement in shipped orientation. The model itself only ever saw the courtyard rectangles.
  • Not modelled: routing/copper (the morph's entire difficulty), rotation (the model only translates), pours, and same-net contact persistence. A clean sample here is a placement-level existence proof, not a routed board.
