Routing Research — Rubber-Band, Homotopy & Force Fields

Literature behind "jiggle v2": relaxing traces and vias along with parts, instead of rip-up-and-reroute · pulled 2026-08-09 · PDFs live in review/research/

Context: the rev-B corner rework left 214 airwires because the relaxation moved parts and we ripped the copper. The proposal on the table is a connectivity-preserving field — pads anchor trace endpoints, vias and segment nodes are free bodies, clearance is enforced as a constraint each pass, and full DRC runs periodically as ground truth. None of this is a new idea in isolation; below is who did each piece, and what's worth stealing from each.

1 · The direct ancestors: rubber-band & topological routing

Topological Routing in SURF: Generating a Rubber-Band Sketch LINK ONLY

Dai, Dayan & Staepelaere · UC Santa Cruz · DAC 1991 · ACM (paywalled) · Semantic Scholar

The founding paper for exactly our approach: wires modeled as elastic rubber bands stretched between pins, routed topologically first (which side of what does each wire pass) with geometry deferred. Naturally any-angle. The "sketch" is a canonical minimal-length embedding of a fixed topology — which is precisely what our relaxed copper should converge to.

Steal: the two-level split. Topology (crossing order) is data; geometry is just the relaxed embedding of it. Our field only ever changes geometry — that's why it's stable and why it can't fix a bad topology. Companion pieces: SURF system paper (1993), Dayan's thesis "Rubber-Band Based Topological Router" (1997), and tech report UCSC-CRL-92-50 on layer assignment for rubber-band routing.

TopoR — the commercial proof it works LINK ONLY

Eremex (Luzin et al.), lineage from 1988 · Wikipedia · EDN overview

A shipping PCB router with no preferred directions: traces are flexible objects that continuously redistribute as parts move, geometry finalized late as arcs and free angles. Known for shortening total wire length and cutting via count vs. grid routers. The Luzin papers are in Russian-language journals and not freely available, but the product documentation describes the behavior well.

Steal: the workflow insight — in TopoR you can drag a component after routing and the copper follows. That is exactly the "replay placement moves, drag copper" plan for the corner.

2 · The math that says when it's possible: homotopy

Single-Layer Wire Routing and Compaction BOOK

F. Miller Maley · MIT Press 1990 · MIT Press

The rigorous treatment of our regime: wires as flexible connections with fixed topology, layouts legalized by continuous deformation (homotopy). Maley's key result: a homotopic routing is realizable iff every "cut" between obstacles has enough capacity for the wires that must cross it — checkable without actually drawing the geometry.

Steal: cut capacity as a fast feasibility oracle. Before iterating 400 passes, check the pinch points (e.g. between a pad field and the board edge): if width-needed exceeds width-available, no amount of jiggling converges — that spot needs a topology change (via hop) up front, not relaxation.

Testing Homotopy for Paths in the Plane LINK ONLY

Cabello, Liu, Mantler & Snoeyink · SoCG 2002 · ACM

Efficient algorithms to decide whether two paths are homotopic (deformable into each other without crossing obstacles).

Steal: a post-run invariant check. After relaxation, verify each dragged trace is homotopic to its pre-rip original relative to pinned obstacles — i.e. prove the field never changed topology, only shape. Cheap insurance that "keeps traces connected" also means "kept them equivalent."

3 · Solver machinery: constraints & negotiation

Position Based Dynamics LINK ONLY

Müller, Heidelberger, Hennix & Ratcliff · J. Vis. Commun. 2007 · Elsevier

The games/graphics standard for simulating constrained systems (cloth, ropes) without force blow-ups: skip forces for hard limits, move positions directly, then run Gauss–Seidel projection over all constraints each step. Unconditionally stable, handles inequality constraints (≥ clearance) natively.

Steal: the whole solver core. Traces = PBD ropes (distance constraints along the segment chain), clearance = inequality constraints projected every pass, pad attachments = infinite-mass anchors. This is the "enforce basic DRC concepts at every pass" mechanism, and it's why v2 shouldn't be springs-only like v1.

PathFinder: A Negotiation-Based Performance-Driven Router LINK ONLY

McMurchie & Ebeling · FPGA 1995 · ACM

The classic iterative scheme behind most modern routers: let nets share resources illegally at first, then raise the cost of each contested resource every iteration until congestion resolves itself. Signals that have alternatives yield; the one that needs the resource most keeps it.

Steal: escalating penalties for the DRC-in-the-loop pass. When full DRC keeps flagging the same clearance violation across runs, don't push harder uniformly — raise that specific constraint's weight each round so the loser reroutes around it. Negotiation, not brute force.

4 · The learning-based corner

Idea noted (2026-08-09): Sequoia wants to see what a fresh placement of this board looks like under diffusion-based placement (paper below) — generate placements by iterative denoising with legality + wirelength guidance, as a comparison point against the incremental jiggle approach. Practical route: their guidance potentials (overlap, HPWL) are exactly our courtyard-gap and airwire-length terms, so we could also just run our own field from random initialization as a poor-man's version, then compare. FUTURE EXPERIMENT

Chip Placement with Diffusion PDF

Lee, Deng, Elzeiny, Abbeel & Wawrzynek · UC Berkeley · arXiv 2407.12282 (2024) · local PDF · arXiv

Places all components simultaneously by denoising, rather than sequentially (RL) or by force minimization alone. GNN + attention over the netlist; trained on synthetic data generated by inverting the problem (make a netlist for which a known placement is near-optimal). Guidance potentials for legality and wirelength steer sampling without retraining.

Steal: the guided-sampling framing. Their "universal guidance" potentials are differentiable versions of our constraints — a shared vocabulary between jiggle v2 and a possible future learned placer. Also the synthetic-data trick if we ever train anything.

FanoutNet: Neuralized PCB Fanout via Deep RL LINK ONLY

Li, Zhang, Xu & Liu · AAAI 2023 · AAAI

RL (PPO, CNN + attention) for the fanout/escape-routing stage of dense PCBs — the first automation paper aimed specifically at PCB fanout rather than IC routing.

Steal: problem decomposition. Escape/fanout (getting from a pad field to open board) is a separate, harder subproblem than open-field routing — matches our observation that the encoder-corner pad fields are where relaxation alone will leave a residue.

A Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach for Global Routing LINK ONLY

Liao et al. · arXiv 1906.08809 (2019) · arXiv

DQN over a grid-graph global-routing environment; jointly optimizes net order and paths. More IC-flavored, but a clean reference for formulating routing as sequential decisions with capacity costs.

Steal: mostly context — shows where the RL literature sits vs. our deterministic deformation approach (they solve topology, we solve geometry).

PCBWorld: A Benchmark Environment for Engine-Grounded PCB Design Automation PDF

arXiv 2607.05915 (2026) · local PDF

Recent benchmark environment coupling PCB automation agents to a real design-rule engine — the "ground truth DRC in the loop" philosophy, institutionalized.

Steal: validation of the architecture choice: keep the fast approximate model inside the loop, but let the real engine (for us, pcbnew 9.0.8 DRC) be the referee, periodically.

5 · Nearest in-tool relative

KiCad's push-and-shove router DOCS

Wlostowski & Suminski (CERN) · KiCad docs

Already implements local copper deformation: dragging a trace shoves neighbors while maintaining clearance and connectivity — but only interactively, one object at a time, driven by the mouse.

Steal: the honest benchmark. Jiggle v2 is "push-and-shove where the mouse is a 400-iteration global field." If a corner case resists the field, P&S drag in the GUI is the manual fallback — same physics, human topology decisions.

What this means for jiggle v2

The literature cleanly endorses the plan and sharpens it in four places:

  • PBD, not springs, for clearance and pad-attachment (Müller) — v1's spring/push model stays only for the long-range spreading term.
  • Check cut capacity first (Maley) at the corner pinch points, so we pre-place the few unavoidable via hops instead of asking gradient descent to invent them. Partially done 2026-08-09: Maley's fixed-topology regime is now the reroute stage — jiggle2/reroute.py routes withdrawn clusters by covering-space A* constrained to the pre-rip homotopy class; 149→71 airwires DRC-neutral, see pcb review §8d. The cut-capacity oracle (pre-placing via hops where no class is feasible) is still open — it is exactly what the 37 unroutable terminals need.
  • Escalate per-violation costs across DRC rounds (PathFinder) instead of raising global stiffness.
  • Verify homotopy after (Cabello) — machine-check that every dragged trace kept its topology. Done 2026-08-09: jiggle2/homotopy.py, 722/722 chains pass in both variants — see pcb review §8c.
